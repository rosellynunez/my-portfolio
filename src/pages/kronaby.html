Kronaby Website Redesign

Website Design & Development for "Introduction to Automated Negotiation"

Background

Kronaby is a Swedish hybrid watch brand known for its minimalist design and smart technology. As part of the Festina Group, the brand needed a digital refresh to better fit into Festina's ecosystem and support more consistent, scalable digital experiences.

project overview

The Kronaby website was built on PrestaShop and did not fully align with Festina's digital ecosystem. This caused design and development inefficiencies and limited the site's ability to drive direct traffic and sales.

To address this, we launched a phased redesign and migrated the site into Festina's Bootstrap-based system. This allowed us to improve consistency across brands, streamline workflows, and create a stronger foundation for future growth.

my role

As the UX/UI designer, I led the redesign of Kronaby's website, handling:

  • User research and competitive analysis to identify key issues.
  • Wireframing and prototyping in Figma
  • ollaboration with strategy, marketing, and development teams to ensure smooth implementation

Project Scope & Constraints

To work within Festina's existing platform and its limitations, we took a phased approach based on what could realistically be changed at the time:

  • Phase 1: We focused on elements that could be updated independently, such as the homepage, landing pages, and other key user-facing sections that needed improvement.
  • Phase 2: More complex areas, like product pages and the checkout flow, were planned for a later stage since they were deeply connected to Festina's core systems.

This approach allowed us to make meaningful improvements quickly, without risking disruption to the wider digital ecosystem.

Acting Fast: From Insight to Action

When I joined the project, the dev team unexpectedly had a short window available to implement design updates. This wasn't common, so I had to move fast.

Ideally, I would have run a full research sprint, but time was tight. Instead, I adapted by doing quick validation through guerrilla testing, internal feedback, short user interviews, and competitor research.

It wasn't perfect, but it helped uncover the key issues and move forward with confidence. It was a good reminder that even small, focused research can have real impact.

3. UX Research

Since previous site did not have tracking tools installed, I relied on qualitative methods to uncover the main problems with the website.

Research Goals

Based on my initial observations, I defined three research goals to validate the core problems (and guide early design decisions:)

🎯
  • Improve navigation and usability — Understand how users navigate the site and identify friction points in the structure.
  • Clarify the product concept — Learn how users perceive hybrid watches and where confusion arises.
  • Strengthen visibility — Analyze basic SEO performance and benchmark competitor visibility.

Methods Used

  • Identified key concerns via user and stakeholder interviews.
  • Observed user website interactions.
  • Competitive benchmarking: analyzed industry best practices.
  • Determined usability inconsistencies through content and architecture review.

User interviews & stakeholder discussions

After speaking with users and stakeholders, these were the key comments that emerged:

Users 🧑🏻

  • "What exactly is a 'hybrid' watch?".
  • "The descriptions's texts are too long, I don't want to read long texts"
  • "I didn't understand the features, so I held back."

Stakeholders 🧑🏻‍💼

  • "Our competitors have really good content"
  • "Our sales are lower than expected".
  • "We need to clearly communicate the value proposition of our hybrid watches".

4. Insights and UX Decisions

Insight 1

💡

🚩 Confusing navigation → High drop-off

Findings:

Overlapping menus made it hard for users to find key sections and products.

Impact:

Users left the site early without exploring products.

Decision:

I simplified the navigation, removed repeated links, and reorganized key categories to create a clearer and more intuitive experience.

Insight 2

💡

🚩 Unclear product concept → Missed sales

Findings:

Users did not understand what a hybrid watch is or its value. Benchmark showed that short text and light storytelling improve understanding.

Impact:

A weak value proposition led to low engagement.

Decision:

Added educational visuals and short, scannable text to clearly explain the product concept across main entry points.

Insight 3

💡

🚩 Low Visibility → Few Direct Visits

Findings:

The site had low organic visibility. Benchmark research showed that strong SEO content combined with clear brand storytelling improves discoverability.

Impact:

Low awareness resulted in fewer direct visits.

Decision:

I worked with an SEO expert to optimize content, improve headings, and add guides for rankings.

Visual

Typography: Balancing aesthetics and performance

Kronaby's style guide specified Avenir Next and Minion fonts, But the website was a mess! They were using Avenir Next, Lucida Grande, Iconochive, and sometimes even Roboto when the others weren't working. This inconsistency also impacted performance, as some of these fonts were not available on Google Fonts. So, I fixed it by switching to DM Sans. It's clean, kinda like Avenir Next, and it made the site look consistent and load faster.

This image showcases the inconsistent and chaotic typography found on the original Kronaby website. Notice the jarring mix of Avenir Next, Lucida Grande, Iconochive, and even Roboto used throughout the site. This not only disrupted the visual flow but also hindered the website's performance.

Color Palette

Kronaby's brand identity is rooted in elegance and innovation, which I translated into a refined color scheme:

  • Primary Colors: A mint green (#417178), representing precision and technology, paired with a complementary brown (#785954) that adds warmth.
  • Secondary Palette: Developed by adjusting the saturation and luminosity of the primary colors, creating depth and visual variety.
  • Semantic Colors: Used strategically to enhance usability and reinforce key actions.

Application of colors

On the product grid page, I applied the brand colors to enhance clarity and engagement:

  • Background: Mint-cool-100 provides a subtle, elegant foundation.
  • Typography & Icons: Mint-cool-900, ensuring strong contrast and readability.
  • Interactive Elements: Brown-vivid-500 as an accent for interactive icons like the “like” button, creating a visually cohesive experience.

7. Collaboration with multiple teams

Bringing Kronaby's brand to life online required close collaboration with multiple teams across different locations:

  • Spain (Marketing Team): Ensured the new website aligned with Festina Group's ecosystem.
  • Sweden (Strategic Team): Maintained consistency between the website and Kronaby's mobile app experience.

Bi-weekly meetings kept us aligned and the project moving forward. I acted as a bridge between design, marketing, and strategy teams, facilitating decision-making and maintaining a user-centric approach throughout the process.

8. Final Results

19. What about metrics?

I wrapped up this project in my last week at Festina Group, so I didn't get the chance to track final results. If I had stayed longer, I would have looked at things like how long users stayed on the site, how fast they left, how many came directly, and whether the clearer UI encouraged more people to start checkout. Even without the final numbers, thinking about these metrics helped guide my design decisions.

10. Next steps for Kronaby

Honestly, the big thing would be tracking user behavior and refine the site. More engaging content would boost SEO and explain the product. Building a community with reviews and forums would be great. And of course, tying it all in better with the rest of Festina's world, especially that checkout redesign, would make the whole experience smoother.

11. Key takeaways & learnings

  • You don't always need analytics to get useful insights—qualitative research can still give a lot of value.
  • Working with tight deadlines means being flexible and adapting the research process when needed.
  • Close collaboration with developers is key to finding solutions that work for both design and technical limits.
  • Instead of trying to fix everything, focusing on the most impactful and realistic changes makes a bigger difference.
  • SEO isn't optional—it should be part of the UX thinking from day one.
