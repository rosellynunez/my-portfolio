Kronaby Website Redesign

Website Design & Development for "Introduction to Automated Negotiation"

Background

Kronaby is a Swedish hybrid watch brand that combines minimalist design with smart technology. As part of the Festina Group, the brand needed its digital presence to better match the group's standards and support future growth.

project overview

The existing website was built on PrestaShop and did not fit well within Festina's digital ecosystem. This created inefficiencies in design and development.

To address this, we launched a phased redesign and migrated the site into Festina's Bootstrap-React system, improving consistency across brands.

my role

As the UX/UI designer, I led the website redesign, from research and wireframing to prototyping in Figma. I collaborated closely with strategy, marketing, and development teams to bring the designs to life.

Working within constraints: Project Scope

To work within Festina's existing platform, we took a phased approach based on what could realistically be changed. Some areas could be updated independently, while others were deeply tied to core systems.

  • Phase 1: We focused on elements that could be updated independently, such as the homepage, landing pages, and other key user-facing sections.
  • Phase 2: More complex areas like product pages and checkout, planned for a later stage.

3. UX Research

When I joined the project, the development team had a short window to implement design updates, which meant moving fast. Ideally, I would have run a full research sprint, but with limited time, I focused on quick validation through guerrilla testing, internal feedback, short interviews, and competitor research.

Since the previous site did not have tracking tools installed, I relied mainly on qualitative methods to understand the main problems with the website.

Early assumptions

Based on early observations, internal feedback, and an initial review of the site, I identified a few assumptions about where users might struggle. For example, the navigation felt confusing, the product concept was not clearly explained, and the site had low visibility. These were assumptions, not conclusions.

Research Goals

These assumptions helped define the research focus. The goal was to validate whether these issues were real and understand them better:

  • Navigation & usability: Understand how users move through the site and where friction appears
  • Product understanding: Learn how users perceive hybrid watches and where confusion arises
  • Visibility: Review basic SEO performance and benchmark competitor visibility

Methods Used

To validate these assumptions, I used a mix of qualitative methods:

  • User and stakeholder interviews
  • Observation of user interactions
  • Content and information architecture review
  • Competitive benchmarking across similar and adjacent industries

Competitive benchmarking

To better understand best practices, I analyzed minimalist websites from direct and indirect competitors, as well as e-commerce brands outside the watch category. This helped compare Kronaby's UX against luxury, mid-range, and conversion-driven experiences.

Usability inconsistencies

Alongside interviews, a quick review of the existing website revealed several usability inconsistencies.

1-Before

The previous Kronaby desktop homepage had a confusing two-menu structure. The main menu mixed watch collections with an app marketplace that was not a priority. A secondary menu repeated links and added extra information. This made navigation less clear and partially covered the image slider.

After

The redesigned homepage simplifies navigation by focusing the main menu on watch collections and grouping supporting content more clearly. Redundant links were removed, and the image slider is now fully visible, creating a cleaner and more user-friendly experience.

3-Before

The old Kronaby webpage used a yellow color that didn't match the brand style and showed very few lifestyle images. Product features were explained with too much text. In addition, the main product photos were not clearly visible, making it harder to focus on the watch.

After

The redesigned homepage simplifies navigation by focusing the main menu on watch collections and grouping supporting content more clearly. Redundant links were removed, and the image slider is now fully visible, creating a cleaner and more user-friendly experience.

4. Insights and UX Decisions

Based on research findings and usability observations, I grouped the issues into clear insights and translated them into design decisions.

Insight 1

🚩 Users Get Lost in Navigation → High drop-off

Finding: Overlapping menus made it hard for users to find key sections and products.

Impact: Users left the site early without exploring products.

Decision: I simplified the navigation, removed repeated links, and reorganized key categories to create a clearer and more intuitive experience.

Insight 2

🚩 Users don't understand Hybrid Watches → Missed sales

Finding: Users did not understand what a hybrid watch is or its value. Benchmark showed that short text and light storytelling improve understanding.

Impact: A weak value proposition led to low engagement.

Decision: I added educational visuals and short, scannable text across key entry points to clearly explain the product concept.

Insight 2

🚩 Low visibility → Low awareness

Findings: The site had weak organic visibility. Benchmarking showed that SEO content and clear storytelling help users discover and understand the brand.

Impact: Low awareness resulted in fewer direct visits.

Decision: I collaborated with an SEO expert to improve content structure, headings, and add SEO-focused guides.

Typography: Balancing aesthetics and performance

Kronaby's style guide specified Avenir Next and Minion fonts, But the website was a mess! They were using Avenir Next, Lucida Grande, Iconochive, and sometimes even Roboto when the others weren't working. This inconsistency also impacted performance, as some of these fonts were not available on Google Fonts. So, I fixed it by switching to DM Sans. It's clean, kinda like Avenir Next, and it made the site look consistent and load faster.

Color palette

Kronaby's brand identity is rooted in elegance and innovation, which I translated into a refined color scheme:

  • Primary Colors: A mint green (#417178), representing precision and technology, paired with a complementary brown (#785954) that adds warmth.
  • Secondary Palette: Developed by adjusting the saturation and luminosity of the primary colors, creating depth and visual variety.
  • Semantic Colors: Used strategically to enhance usability and reinforce key actions.

8. Final Results

19. What about metrics?

I wrapped up this project in my last week at Festina Group, so I didn't get the chance to track final results. If I had stayed longer, I would have looked at things like how long users stayed on the site, how fast they left, how many came directly, and whether the clearer UI encouraged more people to start checkout. Even without the final numbers, thinking about these metrics helped guide my design decisions.

10. Next steps for Kronaby

Honestly, the big thing would be tracking user behavior and refine the site. More engaging content would boost SEO and explain the product. Building a community with reviews and forums would be great. And of course, tying it all in better with the rest of Festina's world, especially that checkout redesign, would make the whole experience smoother.

11. Key takeaways & learnings

  • You don't always need analytics to get useful insights—qualitative research can still give a lot of value.
  • Working with tight deadlines means being flexible and adapting the research process when needed.
  • Close collaboration with developers is key to finding solutions that work for both design and technical limits.
  • Instead of trying to fix everything, focusing on the most impactful and realistic changes makes a bigger difference.
  • SEO isn't optional—it should be part of the UX thinking from day one.
